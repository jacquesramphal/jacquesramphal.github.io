# Capstone: ship a design system with agents

_Not a reading lesson. This is the build. Ship the thing, then write the one paragraph that makes it argue for you._

Everything the course taught was a part of one move, and the capstone is where you make the whole move at once. You take the thing you picked in the first lesson and you finish it: a small design system, built in code, tokenized so it themes by values, generated with agents you kept on guardrails, and triaged down to only what belongs. Then you ship it somewhere real and write the paragraph that explains why the decisions in it were right.

The finished thing is what matters here. You already know the moves; this lesson is the pressure that turns them into something you can show.

## What "done" means here

Done is not "I understand how this would work." Done is deployed, reachable by a link, and honest about its scope. Small and finished beats large and abandoned every time, and a themeable card set that actually ships is worth more than a sprawling system that lives forever in a branch.

Concretely, the capstone holds all five moves the course built:

- It's in code, and you can read and steer every part of it.
- It runs on tokens, and you can reskin it by changing values, not components.
- It was generated with agents, and your guardrails are in the repo where anyone can see the rules you held them to.
- It's been triaged, and you can point at what you cut and say why.
- It's presented as evidence, not a deliverable dump.

## The paragraph

When it's shipped, write one paragraph: why the decisions mattered. Not what you built, because anyone can see what you built. Why this token structure and not another. Why you cut the thing you cut. Why you trusted the agent here and overrode it there. This paragraph is the capstone's real deliverable, because it's the one part a generator couldn't have produced for you.

If you want a hard read on it, this is the point to bring in a paid feedback review: someone senior looking at the shipped thing and the paragraph together, telling you whether the judgment reads as judgment. That's optional, and it's the closest thing this course has to a grade.

## Come back to your line

Find the line you wrote before lesson one, the one thing you wanted to be able to do that you couldn't. Read it now, against the shipped thing.

If the capstone is that line made real, you're done, and you're now demonstrably in the tier the course was about. If it isn't quite, you know exactly what the gap is, which is its own kind of finished.

The finished, shown thing is the whole argument.
