# Reposition yourself

_About ten minutes, then a rewrite of your own headline and one case study. Turn a deliverable list into evidence of judgment._

By now you can steer code, own a token system, direct agents, and run a drift audit and defend it. The last gap is that your public face, your site and headline and case studies, probably still presents the designer you were rather than the one you've become. If the page says "I make screens," it's advertising the tier that's getting cheaper.

Repositioning is not a new coat of adjectives. You don't need "AI-powered" or "design engineer" bolted onto a bio that still lists deliverables underneath. The words are easy to claim and everyone is claiming them. What's hard to fake, and therefore worth showing, is evidence: a decision you made, the constraint you made it under, and what it cost or saved. Judgment leaves a trail, and repositioning is making that trail the thing people see first.

## From deliverable list to evidence

Take a weak headline: "Senior Product Designer — Figma, prototyping, design systems." It's a list of tools, and a machine can hold most of that list now. Rewrite it toward the thing the machine can't hold: "I decide what ships when anything can be built." Same person, different tier. One advertises execution, the other advertises judgment.

Now a case study. The common shape is a deliverable tour: here's the research, here's the wireframes, here's the final UI, here are the shiny screens. It proves you produced things. It doesn't prove you decided anything. Rewrite it as a decision: here was the constraint, here were the options, here's the one I chose and the because, here's what it cost the ones I cut. The screens can stay, but they become evidence for the decision instead of the point of the page.

A reader skimming the first version learns you're busy. A reader skimming the second learns you're accountable. Only one of those is scarce.

## Your turn

Do it to your own. Take your real headline and rewrite it once, moving it off the tool list and onto the judgment. Then take one real case study, your best one, and restructure it around the decision instead of the deliverables. Lead with the constraint. Name the options. Defend the choice. Let the screens be proof, not payload.

Read both versions cold the next day. If the new one sounds like a claim you'd have to back up in a room full of skeptics, it's working. If it still reads as a list of what you can operate, go again.

A designer who writes production code is easy to claim. Make the page the proof.
